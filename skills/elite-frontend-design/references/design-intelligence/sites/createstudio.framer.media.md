Evidence is saved in modules/distilled-web-toolkit/data/createstudio-*-2026-10-16.csv: route inventory **31 rows** (21 official + 10 supplementary), all same-domain links, **44 rendered Chromium states** (22 paths including /404 at desktop **1440x900** and mobile **390x844**) and **12 bounded interaction probes**. ## Visual Identity — Swiss Editorial with Dynamic Presentation

Near-white computed body background **rgb(242,242,242), #F2F2F2**; deep near-black typography, restrained gray utility text, occasional orange/red brand cues or rich portfolio imagery. **Figtree** is the actual computed heading family, not Inter. Display language uses sweeping graphic scale, editorial whitespace, modular Swiss-like grids, dotted degree/section counters **// 00.01°**, micro utility labels, mixed black/gray/white frames, large photography and video showreel.

Desktop homepage H1 computed about **121px**, /work and /studio headings about **160px**; the body DOM has **99 image nodes, six video nodes and zero canvas** on desktop, **252 images, seven video nodes and zero canvas** at 390px. Mobile DOM includes duplicated responsive media/heading variants, not a claim that 252 separate images downloaded. Homepage document is **29,328px desktop and 31,347px mobile**. Other sampled /work and /studio H1 compute 160px even on phone, yet no outer overflow: this can indicate cropped/scaled editorial type or variant visibility; do **not** prescribe 160px as a universal mobile H1. H1 counts across routes vary: /contact, /whispers and legal pages have **no H1 in sampled DOM**, while some long project pages put their branded name in H2 and later generic CTA as H1. Improve semantic structure on reuse.

No canvas nodes were observed across render samples. Site nevertheless contains video and motion choreography, so require reduced-motion/static poster behavior and measure performance independently. Code frameworks (Next.js/WebGL/React) listed as **example case-study technology tags** do not prove implementation of the template itself.

Twelve bounded browser probes cover work archive visibility (3/5 vs published 5), search form/Project Name field, search Aurelis without confirmed href reduction, Brand & Identity category changed project member set but count stayed three, Whispers seven-article archive and search input with unconfirmed result reduction, Contact visible fields/native validity, homepage Core/Studio/Scale CTA link mapping, Aurelis detail chapter headings, two Terms path content/canonical comparison and mobile navigation controls.

## Promotable Mechanisms

**Swiss Type + Human Story Rhythm**: giant Figtree manifestos, precise tiny degree counters, strong image/reel transitions, black/white structural rhythm and one clear work/contact link per stage.
**Long-form Case as Attributed Story**: CMS client/year/role/brief/challenge/solution/results/credits and real live-source proof, not borrowed fixture logos.
**Selected Projects versus Whole Directory**: mark three featured examples as curated and expose all five officially published case details elsewhere or via index.
**Actual Search Result Contract**: project/blog search and category must visibly change matching card subset, expose count/reset/empty state, and retain reachable detail pages.
**Project Package Ladder and Honesty**: three pricing tiers with scope/revision/timeline and real CTA, no claim that subscription billing or checkout is confirmed.
**Real-time Presence with Source Clock**: clock based on IANA timezone, availability from live schedule/explicitly updated copy, no past "next available" date.
**Single Business Identity and Correct Canonical**: one registered address/email, unique legal canonical, valid nav, proper accessible heading hierarchy.
**Graceful Rich Media on Mobile**: choose focal framing, reduce motion and avoid duplicated responsive loads; counted media nodes are not transfer/bandwidth measurements.
**Template Publisher Proof Firewall**: replace sample awards/metrics/testimonials/business entities and eliminate marketplace storefront references unless intentionally representing the publisher.
