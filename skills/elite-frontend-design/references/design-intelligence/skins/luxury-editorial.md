# Baseline Skin: Luxury Editorial

## Use when
Use for fashion, architecture, hospitality, luxury goods, culture, beauty, automotive, or art where pacing, imagery, and typography should carry more weight than feature density.

## Deterministic Design Scaffold
Luxury comes from selective emphasis, material/image quality, and restraint—not from mechanically adding gold, serif type, or whitespace.

## Token Scaffold
- one dominant neutral family + one restrained accent/material cue;
- mostly open page; cards are exceptional;
- hairline low-contrast borders; media radius 0–10px; controls 8–14px;
- container 1240–1440px; section gap 128–200px;
- display 64–108px desktop; body 16–19px with generous leading.

## Composition Grammar
- use image/type tension, asymmetry, crop, and negative space before card systems;
- one product/story per major viewport when practical;
- proof may be craft, provenance, material, press, cultural context, or product detail;
- conversion must not collapse the editorial rhythm.

## Skin Lock
Lock typography roles, neutral palette logic, media treatment, spacing rhythm, control language, and sparse motion.

## Controlled Mutation
May mutate imagery, accent material, hero crop/composition, storytelling order, and one signature transition.

## Responsive Contract
Preserve image/type hierarchy and premium pacing while reducing empty space enough to remain usable on mobile.

## Anti-Template Guard
Do not equate luxury with serif + beige + giant whitespace; the differentiator must come from real product/material/story truth.

## QA Rubric
Verify crop quality, text measure, negative-space intent, restrained surfaces, contrast, mobile pacing, and product-specific material identity.
