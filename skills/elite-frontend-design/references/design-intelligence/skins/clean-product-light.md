# Baseline Skin: Clean Product Light

OrbAI audited template anchor: ../sites/orbai-template.framer.website.md

This skin is for clear, approachable product/service marketing where a light shell, crisp hierarchy, restrained cards, simple proof, and conversion clarity matter more than cinematic spectacle.

OrbAI contributes an audited full-site baseline for:
- light B2B/service conversion architecture;
- Commercial Core + Trust Satellites;
- asymmetric feature/service card systems;
- service-process storytelling;
- pricing followed by defensible comparison;
- contact/privacy/changelog satellite routes;
- responsive card stacking and readable mobile/legal states.

Do not copy OrbAI’s exact Satoshi/Inter pairing, purple accent, ambient video, AI copy, template clients, project metrics, pricing, team names, release history, or Framer chrome.

## Use when

Use for:
- focused SaaS/product marketing;
- productized services;
- AI/automation agencies;
- B2B service firms;
- approachable software;
- early-stage products that fit one strong commercial page;
- lower-capability implementations that need a deterministic light scaffold.

Prefer enterprise-ai-platform-light.md when the product needs broad enterprise route depth, operational pipeline proof, security/legal complexity, careers, integrations, and deeper trust architecture.

Prefer developer-infra.md when code/API proof is primary.

## Deterministic Design Scaffold

Use:
- one calm light shell;
- one brand accent family;
- one clear promise;
- product/service evidence every few sections;
- consistent 16-20px card family;
- familiar conversion primitives;
- one commercial core;
- only the satellite routes that deserve independent depth.

The scaffold should remain coherent before bespoke art direction is added.

## Token Scaffold

- page shell: #f5f5f5-class soft neutral or adjacent off-white;
- primary surface: white or same neutral with clear separation;
- ink: near-black;
- muted copy: accessible medium neutral;
- border: low-contrast neutral hairline;
- brand accent: one saturated family; do not default to OrbAI #814fff;
- optional dark/deep accent: one near-black or brand-dark surface;
- card radius: 16-20px;
- compact control radius: 8-14px;
- pill actions only when they fit the identity;
- shallow shadow or none;
- content max-width: roughly 1160-1240px;
- section gap desktop: roughly 96-144px;
- section gap mobile: roughly 64-96px;
- major heading desktop: roughly 48-64px;
- major heading mobile: roughly 32-40px;
- large identity display may exceed this only when it owns a clear visual role;
- body: roughly 15-18px with readable line length.

OrbAI evidence shows 56px-class section headings desktop -> 36px mobile, 20px rounded feature/service cards, and 16px pricing cards. Treat these as a coherent baseline range, not mandatory values.

## Typography Roles

Recommended:
- identity/display role;
- section heading role;
- body/UI role;
- optional data/price role.

OrbAI uses Inter for its giant ORB AI identity and Satoshi for most marketing hierarchy. Transfer the separation of roles, not the font names.

Avoid adding mono or handwritten fonts merely to simulate "AI" or "startup" aesthetics.

## Composition Grammar

### Commercial core / home

A reliable focused-site sequence is:

1. positioning / hero;
2. short credibility or thesis statement;
3. benefits;
4. feature/capability explanation;
5. service/product offer;
6. process or how-it-works when execution risk matters;
7. projects/results/product proof;
8. testimonials/social proof;
9. pricing or engagement model;
10. comparison / differentiation when defensible;
11. human/team trust when relevant;
12. FAQ;
13. final conversion/footer.

Do not treat this order as mandatory. Delete stages that do not answer a real buyer question.

### Hero

- expose the job/promise immediately;
- keep primary + secondary action hierarchy obvious;
- use ambient media only when it supports identity;
- do not let decorative media become fake product evidence;
- preserve a usable static/reduced-motion state.

### Benefits

Use compact cards when each card answers one buyer outcome.

Keep:
- one outcome;
- one short explanation;
- one small supporting visual or datum.

Do not make every claim a separate card if the ideas belong together.

### Feature / capability bento

Asymmetric bento is allowed when information weight differs.

Good:
- one wide visual/explanation;
- one narrow companion;
- reverse or rebalance in the next row when the content supports it;
- consistent radius and vertical rhythm.

Bad:
- random card spans for visual novelty;
- every section rendered as bento;
- fake dashboard visuals used as evidence.

### Service / offer bento

For agencies and service businesses:
- service name;
- short outcome;
- what the work actually includes;
- illustrative example only when honest;
- link/CTA only when there is a deeper action.

A service card is not a substitute for explaining delivery.

### Process

Use 3-5 steps when buyers need to understand how work proceeds.

Typical:
1. assess/discover;
2. design/build/deploy;
3. support/optimize.

Keep steps causal and specific to the actual service.

### Proof / projects

Prefer:
- named situation;
- concise intervention;
- one or two observable outcomes;
- media or artifact;
- optional case-study route.

Never invent percentages, customers, or outcomes to make a template feel complete.

## Commercial Core + Trust Satellites

Use one commercial core when:
- features/services/pricing/projects fit naturally as sections;
- the primary buying journey benefits from continuous scroll;
- separate routes would mainly duplicate copy.

Use satellite routes for:
- contact;
- privacy/legal;
- changelog/updates;
- help/docs;
- other trust/support material needing independent depth.

From satellites, link back to meaningful commercial-core anchors rather than duplicating the full sales page.

Do not over-expand route count to imply company maturity.

## Pricing

Use 2-4 plans only when pricing truly supports tiers.

Each plan should show:
- who it is for;
- price/engagement basis;
- included value;
- key limits/differences;
- clear next action.

Use one recommended/popular state at most unless there is a strong reason.

On mobile, stack plans and preserve capability differences rather than shrinking a desktop row.

## Pricing-to-Comparison Bridge

After pricing, add comparison only when the visitor still needs to understand why this offer beats a real alternative.

Use:

plan / offer -> included value -> comparison criteria -> objection resolution -> CTA

Comparison criteria should be:
- relevant;
- symmetric;
- specific;
- supportable.

Prefer real buyer differences such as:
- manual vs automated workflow;
- generic vs tailored scope;
- batch vs real-time;
- included vs excluded support;
- limited vs scalable capacity;
- verifiable integration/governance/delivery differences.

Do not use a vague "Us vs Others" table with invented competitor weaknesses.

When direct comparison is inappropriate, use:
- buyer-fit guide;
- scope matrix;
- FAQ;
- "when this plan is not for you" guidance.

## Social Proof

Testimonials, customer logos, team profiles, and metrics must be truthful.

Do not fill template proof slots with fabricated:
- companies;
- quotes;
- roles;
- revenue/growth figures;
- satisfaction percentages;
- case-study outcomes.

If the real product has little social proof, use product/service evidence and process clarity instead.

## Changelog / Updates

A small changelog can be a useful trust satellite when releases are real.

Index:
- date;
- category;
- release/version;
- short summary.

Detail:
- what is new;
- secondary improvements;
- fixes;
- related/previous release.

Do not create fake release history to simulate longevity.

## Privacy / Legal

Keep the same shell, but lower visual intensity.

Requirements:
- readable line length;
- explicit section hierarchy;
- visible last-updated date when relevant;
- business-specific/legal-reviewed content;
- no marketing claims inserted into legal copy.

## Contact

Reduce friction.

Use:
- clear reason to contact;
- only necessary fields;
- readable labels/placeholders;
- visible submit state;
- success/error feedback;
- touch-safe sizing.

Optional FAQ may follow when it resolves common pre-contact objections.

## Ambient Media Bookend

Optional.

A repeated ambient visual in hero + footer can create identity continuity without adding a new design system.

Rules:
- media is decorative unless it shows real product/service evidence;
- clip it inside viewport-safe regions;
- keep muted/autoplay behavior respectful;
- preserve posters/static fallbacks;
- do not keep expensive media active when it adds no value;
- never let it cause document-level overflow.

## Skin Lock

Lock:
- light neutral shell;
- surface hierarchy;
- one accent family;
- typography roles;
- card radius family;
- spacing rhythm;
- primary/secondary action language;
- proof truthfulness;
- Commercial Core + Trust Satellites decision;
- pricing/comparison truth contract;
- mobile stacking behavior;
- restrained motion.

## Controlled Mutation

May mutate:
- accent hue;
- exact neutral temperature;
- typography families;
- hero alignment;
- section order;
- card asymmetry;
- illustration/product media style;
- number of services/features;
- pricing structure;
- signature ambient media;
- whether comparison/team/changelog exists.

Must mutate away from audited identity:
- ORB AI logo/name;
- exact #814fff purple;
- exact Satoshi + Inter pairing;
- exact ambient MP4;
- AI-specific copy;
- project/client/team identities;
- exact plan prices;
- sample release/legal content;
- Framer platform/editor chrome.

## Responsive Contract

Preserve:
- semantic section order;
- offer/proof hierarchy;
- readable typography roles;
- primary/secondary actions;
- pricing differences;
- comparison meaning;
- legal/contact readability.

Adapt:
- 3-column cards -> one column;
- asymmetric bento -> stacked cards or simple alternating blocks;
- desktop display scale -> mobile readable scale;
- comparison columns -> sequential or bounded comparison;
- oversized ambient media -> clipped/static/reduced alternative;
- navigation -> compact mobile menu.

Required:
- no unintended document-level horizontal overflow;
- full-width forms remain inside viewport;
- touch targets remain usable;
- card text does not become tiny merely to preserve desktop geometry.

## Motion Language

Priority:
1. input/CTA feedback;
2. menu/FAQ/carousel state;
3. product/service state when real;
4. restrained enter transitions;
5. ambient identity media.

Avoid:
- universal float animation;
- parallax on every section;
- fake AI "intelligence" expressed only through glow/motion;
- continuous media where a still would communicate equally well.

## Anti-Template Guard

High-risk light AI/service template bundle:
- centered hero;
- purple/blue accent;
- 20px rounded bento cards;
- generic AI feature icons;
- three pricing tiers;
- "Us vs Others" comparison;
- testimonial cards;
- team carousel;
- FAQ;
- glowing/ambient orb;
- dark/video footer.

Any individual pattern can be valid. Shipping the whole bundle unchanged creates Template Convergence Risk.

Require differentiation in at least one structural layer:
- real proof mechanism;
- information model;
- hero composition;
- type system;
- service/process model;
- comparison model;
- interaction grammar;
- route architecture;
- responsive transformation.

Changing only copy and purple hue does not count.

## QA Rubric

Pass only if:
- the main promise is understandable in the first viewport;
- benefits/features/services have distinct jobs;
- card hierarchy follows information weight;
- process explains real delivery;
- proof is evidence-backed;
- pricing matches real packaging;
- Pricing-to-Comparison Bridge uses defensible criteria;
- Commercial Core + Trust Satellites avoids needless duplication;
- contact/privacy/changelog remain useful standalone routes;
- template placeholder content is excluded;
- mobile stacking preserves hierarchy;
- document-level overflow remains controlled;
- ambient media is bounded and non-essential;
- the result still feels intentional after removing OrbAI’s exact fonts, purple, video, copy, pricing, metrics, and AI iconography.
